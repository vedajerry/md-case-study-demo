01 · The problem

What the client asked, and what it meant

The record says it plainly: “Cogitare Learning asked us to initially design their website. Very soon the project also became a rebranding project.”

The reason: the old logo leaned on a butterfly, marking the transformation a student undergoes with Cogitare — but after years of use it had grown heavy. A brand about metamorphosis that looks dated asks the wrong question of a parent comparing coaching options.

And the website could not explain their method on its own — the method is the brand. So the two jobs had to move together.

What we were told to keep

The butterfly — the transformation idea itself. Not its styling. The brief: simplify it, make it geometric, use younger and brighter colour, and set it in geometric, round letterforms.

02 · The outcome at a glance

The three business goals — and what each one revealed

This project has no before/after screens to show — the engagement was led by goals, and the record was the work itself. So its page leads with the three goals the business set, and what solving each one showed us.

1Goal · clarity
A parent should grasp the method in one look

Coaching decisions are made by parents scanning options quickly. The butterfly had to survive the refresh — but arrive modern, geometric and legible at small sizes, on the site and in print.

Insight: a transformation story works only when it stays legible — the motif was kept, its drawing was rebuilt from simple geometry.
1 motif kept · redrawn in geometric shapes
2Goal · energy
The brand should feel like a learner’s world, not an institution’s

The palette switched to younger, brighter colour — orange carrying movement and transformation, blue carrying the processing and reflection that follows it. Letterforms made geometric and round.

Insight: colour can carry meaning, not just mood — orange is the leap, blue is the thinking after it.
2-colour system · meaning built in
3Goal · one system
Website, brochure and identity move as one

The engagement started as web design and grew into full identity — the point being that the site, the print brochure and the brand suite needed to speak one language from day one.

Insight: sequencing matters — brand first, then the website and brochure inherit it, and nothing drifts.
1 system → web + brochure + full identity suite

06 · How AI helped · Echo & Labs

Two assistants inside the studio — and where they were used on Cogitare

We say plainly where our tools sit. Neither designs for the client — both make the studio’s stages faster and every claim checkable. Cogitare ran 2020–21; Echo and Labs are how the studio runs the same sequence today, with each step logged.

Stages 1–2 · Interrogate → Evidence

Echo

Retrieval and evidence logging — research and synthesis in days instead of weeks, every claim carrying a source.

Where it works on a project like Cogitare: the decision to grow a website project into a rebrand is an evidence call — Echo mines the brand’s aged touchpoints against what parents and schools say in comparison conversations, logs both sides, and hands the studio the tested case for expanding scope while the budget still exists.

Stage 3 · Prototype

Labs

Generates and tests interface and identity variants against the criteria named in stage 2 — debates become tested decisions.

Where it works on a project like Cogitare: redraws of the butterfly, orange-weight against blue-weight, geometric vs round letterforms — become scored variants checked against stage-2 criteria (legibility at small sizes, meaning carried by colour) before the mark goes live.

echo · evidenced, logged labs · variant-tested, logged What’s never outsourced: the taste, the judgement, the call — the studio’s.
