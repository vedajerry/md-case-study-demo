01 · The direction, in one line

Buyers don't hire screens. They hire what changed.

Three moves replace the wall of mockups — and each one is built to make a cold buyer decide for us. Fewer screens, shown bigger, with the outcome written on them.

  • 1 — One big screen at a time. Every screen arrives zoomed-in and readable, because in today's frames the buyer squints at the only part that matters. Each screen carries a one-line "what changed" tag — the reason for the screen lives on the screen.
  • 2 — One user story at a time. Three flows, the ones the client's user actually lives ("book a delivery", "get your weekly report"), shown start to finish. Each ends at an outcome the buyer recognises. Journeys sell; screenshots don't.
  • 3 — Before / after. The buyer's own "before" opens the story and our "after" closes it — problems numbered, fixes numbered. Any market, any industry, zero jargon required.
  • Dropped: the next/previous carousel — buyers stop exploring after two clicks and everything after stays unseen. Added: one result line under every section, like 3 tools → 1 · 8 taps → 3 — the five words that close the "so what?"

The new bar for a screen: can a buyer tell our work apart from a template in 5 seconds? If not, it goes back — because the buyer filed us under "template" and left.

Why you can bet on these three

  • They open with the buyer's world, not ours. The comparison starts on the client's current mess. The gallery stops leading with our achievement and starts leading with their problem — that's what earns the next 30 seconds.
  • They argue in numbers, not adjectives. 8 taps → 3 ends debate; "seamless" starts it. Every claim closes the "so what?" in five words or fewer.
  • They sell between meetings. One comparison, one story, one big screen — a buyer with 90 seconds and a phone gets the whole argument without hunting or clicking.
  • They travel without a translation note. No process words on the surface — "your user gets the report on Sunday" reads true in every market. The deeper craft layer (tagged decisions, the ledger) stays one click away for evaluators, and never becomes the front door.

Reviewed with the design team before this deck: Arjun (UX + visual), Anuj (screen density), Meera (buyer read), Priya (build cost on the current stack).

Three reasons the client can't see us in this

  • Half of every mockup is furniture. Classic laptop mockups spend ~45% of their footprint on bezel + desk + shadow. The screen, the only thing that matters, is the minority.
  • Same screen, different words. Product dashboards genuinely share one visual grammar — 3 KPIs, one chart, one table. Side by side, five clients become one picture.
  • Nothing on the screen says "we decided this." What makes our work different is the reasoning; a plain screenshot carries none.

What the client is silently asking

  • "What here only happened because you worked on it?"
  • "Where would I get the same thing from a Figma template?"
  • "If I hire you, what exactly am I buying?"

What changes on the page

  • Before/after goes first, not the polished screenshot. Recognition before admiring.
  • Impact numbers appear once at the top as a whole — then repeated where they happened. No "reveal step by step" animation; the manager who came for the number shouldn't wait for it.
  • Testimonial moves above the appendix, so the last thing a reader sees is proof, not parts.
  • Sticky contents on the right — exactly as planned. On phone they become three small tabs (Problem · Screens · Impact), not a drawer.
  • Every screen on the page carries a tag, one plain sentence each. Nothing ships naked — and no internal jargon reaches the buyer.

Rollout, cheapest-first

  1. Step 1: rebuild the /work gallery — one comparison per case, framed right. Smallest build, most visible change — the site starts selling before anything else moves.
  2. Step 2: upgrade one case study end-to-end as the proof of the whole system.
  3. Step 3: roll the pattern across the remaining cases.

Two honest notes for the meeting

  • The figures in this deck are illustrative — drawn from the placeholder case roster (Meridian / Nimbus / Portside). Before any of this meets a client, screens and numbers need signed-off real work, and anything unmeasured is said so plainly. That honesty is itself part of the pitch — global buyers reward studios that show their working.
  • The screens here are CSS, not images — content changes are text edits, and this prototype doubles as the real gallery's markup. Nothing here needs re-shooting.
