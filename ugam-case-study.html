01 · The problem

What the foundation told us, in their words

The brief was direct: redesign Ugam’s identity. The old one was “becoming very restrictive and structured” — everything sat inside boxes, and the colours were “very dark and strong.”

Meanwhile the brand had to do two jobs at once: feel “aspirational and feminine” to the school girls it exists for in rural India, and stay credible to the donors and stakeholders supporting it from metropolitan cities.

One identity, two very different audiences. That tension is what made the project interesting — and what every decision below answers.

What we were told to keep

Three motifs carried the old brand: a sun, a kite and a pencil. The brief asked for a new logo working in both English and Devanagari (Hindi) — the audience writes in two scripts, the brand must too.

02 · The outcome at a glance

What this project changed

2 scripts, 1 mark One logo that works in English and Devanagari — no separate brand per language. What changed: the box opened, the brand now breathes across both scripts.
3 kept + 1 added Sun, kite and pencil stayed. The sky joined them. Formation, not replacement — continuity from old identity to new.
1 system → 8 touchpoints Website, annual report, brochure, 2 poster campaigns, magazine, newsletter, business cards. Everything after the logo was assembled from one identity.

07 · How AI helped · Echo & Labs

Two assistants inside the studio — and where they were used on Ugam

We say plainly where our tools sat in this project. Neither designs for the client — both make the studio’s stages faster and every claim checkable. Ugam ran 2019–20; Echo and Labs are how the studio runs the same sequence today, with each step logged.

Stages 1–2 · Interrogate → Evidence

Echo

Retrieval and evidence logging — research and synthesis in days instead of weeks, every claim carrying a source.

Where it worked on Ugam: the brief’s core tension — speak to rural KGBV school girls and metro donors at once — is exactly the conflict Echo surfaces in stakeholder language: it mines briefs, interviews and past material, logs what each side said, and hands the studio two tested position statements before a single pixel moves.

Stage 3 · Prototype

Labs

Generates and tests interface and identity variants against the criteria named in stage 2 — debates become tested decisions.

Where it worked on Ugam: briefs like “rotated kite vs static mark, aspirational vs institutional” become Labs variants scored against the two-audience criteria — colour weight tested against photo rules, lockups screened in English and Devanagari before the studio took the mark live.

echo · evidenced, logged labs · variant-tested, logged What’s never outsourced: the taste, the judgement, the call — the studio’s.
